# Creed — Genre and Puzzle (Zuckerman)

## Z1 — A contribution needs a puzzle, and a gap is not one
Paraphrase of Zuckerman ("On Genre," working paper on theory construction in the social sciences): a paper earns its place by resolving a puzzle — an observation that something in the accumulated literature leads us *not* to expect — and not by occupying an unfilled cell. "Nobody has studied X in setting Y" identifies an absence; it does not identify anything that anyone believed and that turns out to be wrong. Absences are infinite and cheap.
*Applies when:* a design's stated motivation is coverage ("understudied", "little is known about"), or when the puzzle page in the ontology attaches to no theoretical expectation.

## Z2 — The genre must be one genre, named
Paraphrase of Zuckerman's genre argument: the recognised paths to contribution are distinct in what they must establish and in what would falsify them, and a paper that mixes them owes each genre's burden without discharging any. A design should be able to say which one it is attempting — and if the honest answer is "some of each", that is a diagnosis, not a strategy.
*Applies when:* a design claims simultaneously to overturn a theory, extend it, and describe a new setting; or when its ontology carries a resolution whose relation to the existing account is left unstated.

## Z3 — The obscured puzzle: familiar, lived, and untheorised
Paraphrase of Zuckerman on why some puzzles stay hidden: a phenomenon can be visible to everyone in a field and still be unexplained, because its very familiarity supplies the *feeling* of understanding. Practitioners navigate the tension every day; scholars share the intuition; nobody has produced a mechanism. These are the most valuable puzzles available and the hardest to see, because the work of noticing them means distrusting one's own fluency.
*Applies when:* a researcher describes a tension that field participants live with routinely, or when their account of it is fluent but produces no mechanism when asked.

## Z4 — Intuition as participants is not theory
Paraphrase of Zuckerman: scholars of social life move between using existing social theory and using their intuition as participants in the social world, and because that intuition works well enough day to day, "we may think we understand it. But even if we understand something at an intuitive level, this does not mean that we have good theory for it." The test is whether the researcher can state what produces what, under which conditions, in what order.
*Applies when:* an explanation is offered as self-evident, or when asking for the mechanism returns an illustration. An example is not a mechanism.

## Z5 — Name whose expectation is violated
Paraphrase of Zuckerman on positioning: a puzzle is always a puzzle *for* someone — a community of scholars who hold a commitment the observation strains. A design that cannot name who is surprised has not established that anyone is. "The literature" is not a someone.
*Applies when:* a puzzle→theory relation is asserted without an expectation node, or when the audience for the surprise is left implicit.

## Z6 — The strength of the claim sets the burden
Paraphrase of Zuckerman on what different framings owe: saying an observation *contradicts* an account demands more than saying it *instantiates* one, and claiming it *exposes* something the account cannot see demands most of all — a tension between accounts, sustained observation, and a consequence that follows. Researchers reach for the strongest available framing because it reads as the biggest contribution, and then cannot pay for it.
*Applies when:* the asserted relation outruns the evidence on the graph; the honest move is a weaker claim the material supports, not a stronger one it might.

## Z7 — Practical tensions are legitimate puzzle roots
Paraphrase extending Zuckerman's puzzle criterion to fieldwork-driven research: a puzzle may originate in a tension actors navigate rather than in a disagreement between published accounts. What makes it a puzzle is unchanged — something must be strained by it — but the strain can run from practice to theory rather than theory to theory. The design's job is to establish which account the practice troubles, not to find a literature that has already declared the tension interesting.
*Applies when:* a researcher has a substantive tension from the field and is casting about for a literature to hang it on, or conversely is suppressing it because no literature names it yet.

## Z8 — Do not assume the tension persists
Paraphrase of the fallibilist commitment implicit in the puzzle criterion: whether a tension endures, resolves, or dissolves under examination is an empirical question about the setting. Importing a framework that presumes persistence answers the question before the fieldwork does, and any finding of endurance then follows from the framing rather than the data.
*Applies when:* a design treats the durability of a tension as given, or when the ontology has no page representing the possibility that the tension resolves.

## Z9 — Resolution must not merely save the theory
Paraphrase of Zuckerman on weak resolutions: an explanation that restores the challenged account without cost is usually a redescription of the anomaly in the account's own vocabulary. A real resolution changes what the theory can claim — narrows its scope, revises a mechanism, or specifies a condition it did not previously carry.
*Applies when:* a candidate resolution leaves the theory it responds to exactly as it was; ask what the theory can no longer say.

## Z10 — Rivals are part of the argument, not an appendix
Paraphrase of Zuckerman on the discipline of contribution: a resolution advanced without a live competitor has been asserted rather than tested, and readers supply the competitor whether or not the author does. Naming the strongest rival account and saying what would distinguish them is what converts a plausible story into a claim.
*Applies when:* a candidate resolution stands alone in the ontology, or when the only rivals recorded are ones the design was never going to support.

## Z11 — Pseudo-genres: apparent contributions that are not
Paraphrase of Zuckerman's warning about contribution forms that mimic the real ones: demonstrating that a well-established relationship also holds in a new setting, opening a black box whose contents nobody disputed, or documenting a phenomenon that troubles no account — each looks like a contribution and none forces revision of anything. They are recognisable by a common feature: after reading, nobody has to believe anything different.
*Applies when:* a design's claimed contribution is replication-in-new-setting, mechanism-elaboration with no disputed outcome, or description of the previously undescribed.

## Z12 — Genre diagnoses; it does not template
Paraphrase of the spirit of the genre argument rather than a specific claim: knowing which contribution logic a design implies is a way of seeing what it currently owes. It is not a form to complete. A researcher who picks a genre first and assembles a study to fit it has inverted the reasoning, and the resulting paper will read as one — the puzzle will have been chosen for its convenience to the frame.
*Applies when:* genre is being used prescriptively; report what the ontology's shape implies and let the researcher disagree with the reading.
