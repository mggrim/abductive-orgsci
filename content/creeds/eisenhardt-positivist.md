# Creed — Case-Study Positivist (Eisenhardt / Yin)

## E1 — Theoretical sampling, not random sampling
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt (1989, "Building Theories from Case Study Research," *Academy of Management Review*): cases in theory-building research should be selected for theoretical reasons — because they replicate previous cases, extend emergent theory, or fill conceptual categories — not by random or convenience sampling. The goal of sampling is to choose cases likely to illuminate and extend relationships among constructs.
*Applies when:* a design justifies site or sample choice by availability, interest, or contrast alone rather than by theoretical purpose.

## E2 — Cases as a series of experiments
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007, "Theory Building from Cases," *Academy of Management Journal*): multiple cases function like a series of experiments, with each case serving to confirm or disconfirm inferences drawn from the others. Theory built from multiple cases is typically more robust, generalizable, and testable than theory from a single case because propositions are grounded in varied empirical evidence.
*Applies when:* a design proposes a single case without justifying why one case can bear the theoretical weight, or proposes multiple cases without saying what each one is for.

## E3 — Literal and theoretical replication
Paraphrase of Yin (*Case Study Research: Design and Methods*): a multiple-case design should follow replication logic, not sampling logic. Each case must be selected either to predict similar results (literal replication) or to predict contrasting results for anticipatable, theoretically specified reasons (theoretical replication). A design that cannot state its replication prediction in advance is not a multiple-case design; it is two unrelated studies.
*Applies when:* a comparative design cannot state, before data collection, what each case is predicted to show and why.

## E4 — Comparing cases that differ on everything tells you nothing
Paraphrase of the polar-types logic in Eisenhardt (1989) and Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007): contrast is only informative when cases are matched on most dimensions so the focal difference can be isolated — polar types are chosen to make a *focal* pattern transparently observable against a controlled background. When two cases differ on industry, occupation, institutional context, and outcome simultaneously, any observed difference is causally uninterpretable.
*Applies when:* a researcher proposes comparing two sites *because* they are different, without holding constant the dimensions that are not under study.

## E5 — Construct validity through triangulation
Paraphrase of Yin's construct-validity tactics (*Case Study Research*): construct validity requires multiple sources of evidence converging on the same construct, a maintained chain of evidence from question to data to conclusion, and key-informant review of draft findings. A construct measured by a single informant's retrospective account in a single data source is not yet a construct; it is an anecdote.
*Applies when:* the data plan relies on one data type or one informant class per construct, with no triangulation strategy.

## E6 — A priori constructs sharpen, they do not bias
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt (1989): specifying potentially important constructs in advance — from the literature — permits more accurate measurement and firmer grounding of emergent theory, provided the researcher holds them as tentative and remains free to discard them. Entering the field with no constructs at all is not open-mindedness; it is a recipe for drowning in data.
*Applies when:* a design brief either refuses to name any expected constructs or, conversely, treats its a priori constructs as fixed conclusions.

## E7 — Retrospective accounts require informant triangulation
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007): the central concern with interview data is retrospective sensemaking and impression management by informants; the mitigation is numerous, knowledgeable informants who view the phenomenon from diverse perspectives — different hierarchical levels, functions, groups, and organizations — plus, where possible, real-time or archival data.
*Applies when:* the data plan is interviews with a homogeneous informant pool recalling events after outcomes are already known.

## E8 — Rival explanations must be designed against
Paraphrase of Yin (*Case Study Research*): internal validity in explanatory case research depends on explicitly identifying and addressing rival explanations — pattern-matching, explanation-building, and logic models exist to rule alternatives out. A design that never names the strongest alternative account of its expected findings has no internal-validity strategy.
*Applies when:* a brief presents one favored explanation and no plan for what evidence would distinguish it from competitors.

## E9 — The single case must be extreme, revelatory, or rare
Paraphrase of Yin's single-case rationales and Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007): single cases earn their keep only when they are unusually revelatory, extreme exemplars, or opportunities for unusual research access; the writer must justify the single case on exactly these grounds. Absent such a rationale, the default presumption favors multiple cases.
*Applies when:* a single-site design is justified by convenience or depth alone without an extremeness, rarity, or access rationale.

## E10 — Overlap data collection and analysis, but discipline the flexibility
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt (1989): overlapping data collection with analysis through field notes and flexible instruments is legitimate — researchers may add data sources or adjust protocols mid-study — but this is controlled opportunism in service of emerging theory, not license to drift. Alterations must be documented and justified by the emerging theoretical need.
*Applies when:* a design promises "emergence" with no protocol at all, or conversely locks its instrument so rigidly that mid-course theoretical sampling is impossible.

## E11 — Enfolding literature: conflict and agreement both required
Paraphrase of Eisenhardt (1989): theory building concludes by comparing emergent theory with conflicting and with similar literature; ignoring conflicting findings undermines confidence, while examining them forces more creative, deeper theory with sharper generalizability claims. A design that only cites supportive literature has skipped half the enfolding step.
*Applies when:* the motivation section cites only work the researcher expects to confirm, and no adjacent literature that would predict otherwise.

## E12 — Access is a design property, not an aspiration
Paraphrase of the field-access discussions in Yin (*Case Study Research*) and Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007): the feasibility of sustained access to the phenomenon is part of case selection itself — unusual research access is a stated rationale for case choice, which implies the access must actually exist before the design is credible. A plan whose evidentiary claims exceed its demonstrated access is unexecutable.
*Applies when:* the data plan requires observation of settings (board meetings, partner meetings, closed negotiations) with no concrete, evidenced access arrangement.
